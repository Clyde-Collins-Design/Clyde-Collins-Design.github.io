Argus

"Argus" plays the dual roles of furniture and setpiece. The unique, sculptural form surrounds the stable wooden base to make a functional chair that gives your living room a unique personality.

Process

The creation of Argus started off rather troubled. I was getting hung up on what the "designerly" thing to do was, because I felt that I was expected to design the way I was taught. But design wouldn't be fun if everyone did the same thing, would it? Early on, I was drawn to very serpentine line figures. Eventually, I found a peculiar shrimp-ish working its way into my sketches. Following this thread, I made a small-scale mockup of this form.


Orestes

Here's where things get fun. The original goal was to create a full-scale mockup of the small 3D shrimp sketch. However, I had other plans. I still wasn't happy with the prospect of making a piece of furniture. No matter how hard I try, I am simply not a furniture designer. And I'm certainly not the average industrial designer. As I started drafing up the cardboard layouts, I began to see this structure not as a piece of furniture, but as a setpiece. A creature. I got more ambitious with the form, adding back fins and a more defined head area. However, once this behemoth, named Orestes, was finished, I knew it was far too big and unwieldy to be fabricated as-is. So it felt like I was back to square one.


Argus Appears

With Orestes being far too impractical, costly, and ungainly to make, I had hit a roadblock. While I was considering what to do next, I absentmindedly drew what I wanted to be making: a creature. Its multiple rows of eye-like orbs running down its figure inspired its name: "Argus". Argus is what I wanted my project to be, and somehow I was going to make it happen.


ARG-1: Argus' Little Big Brother

I have a tendency to think big with my projects, sometimes to my detriment. I knew I wanted Argus to be full-scale and functional as a chair, but first I had to work small. Argus was going to be carved from pink foam, so first I needed to establish a rough form that would utilize the material to its fullest. This was achieved by CAD-modeling a basic form, constrcting it out of stacked, laser-cut slices of cardboard, and making changes where needed.

Using the cardboard form as a reference, I stacked 1" slices of pink foam and began carving. The result was ARG-1: majestic, purple, and destined to be slathered in joint compound. Joint compound was a new material to me, but it could be layered over a carved form to give it a tough exterior. The process was quite effective, but the question still remained: how do I make this into full-scale furniture?


Drafting

The most viable option for fabrication was constructing the seating portion from poplar and MDF, then stacking and joining the pink foam to form the non-structural elements.
